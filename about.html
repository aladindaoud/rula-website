Chapter 01

The Roots of Inquiry

Born between Amman and northern England, Dr. Rula Abdulrazak's earliest intellectual inclinations emerged from the frictions and subtleties of translation. Raised in a household where Arabic poetry resonated against classical Western sociology, she witnessed how entire moral systems mutate when crossing physical and geopolitical thresholds.

She completed her undergraduate studies with double first-class honors at University College London before moving to the University of Oxford, where her doctoral dissertation investigated collective memory, generational grief, and the quiet infrastructures of resilience in post-industrial migration corridors. Her fieldwork involved extended immersion across border enclaves, municipal archives, and oral storytelling rings.

Unlike scholarship confined strictly to numerical telemetry, Dr. Abdulrazak’s approach combines ethnographic immersion with rigorous phenomenological investigation. Her fieldwork established what she terms "the architecture of trust"—the unspoken communal protocols that maintain stability long after legal or state mechanisms recede.

Chapter 02

Bridging the Academy and the Public Sphere

Believing that critical cultural theory should not languish behind paywalled scholarly indexes, Dr. Abdulrazak has committed her mid-career to translational prose. Her second monograph, Friction & Fellowship (2020), was recognized with a National Book Award nomination for its crystalline treatment of how algorithmic curation dismantles civic vulnerability.

Her essays and long-form reportage appear frequently in The Atlantic, The Times Literary Supplement, and Boston Review. Whether delivering keynote addresses to intergovernmental bodies or facilitating intimate seminars with frontline civil servants, her lecturing style is renowned for untangling convoluted cultural dynamics into lucid, actionable moral clarity.

"Sociology is fundamentally an act of moral hospitality," she observed in her widely circulated Geneva forum discourse. "To understand another's socio-structural reality requires laying down the comforting scaffolding of one's own assumptions."

Chapter 03

Current Appointments & Affiliations

Presently, Dr. Abdulrazak serves as Senior Research Fellow in Social Epistemology at the Center for Contemporary Humanities, Cambridge, where she leads the Relational Resilience Lab. Concurrently, she holds a Visiting Professorship in Public Philosophy at the Graduate Institute of International Studies.

Theoretical Axioms

Core Philosophy & Principles

Every inquiry, essay, and keynote is grounded in four cardinal obligations to truth, discourse, and the reader.

01

Empirical Rigor

Rejection of ideological convenience. Grounding speculative social theories inside verifiable archival documentation, longitudinal field interviews, and multi-year cross-cultural datasets.

science Observation First
02

Narrative Clarity

Refusal to treat academic density as an excuse for obfuscation. Cultivating crystalline prose that welcomes non-specialists without flattening the authentic complexity of human affairs.

edit_note Accessible Depth
03

Intellectual Generosity

Approaching contrary perspectives with charitable attention. Seeking the most cogent version of an opposing paradigm before engaging in critical scholarly critique.

handshake Dialectic Respect
04

Civic Responsibility

Theory must ultimately serve democratic life. Generating insights capable of helping everyday communities, educators, and public leaders steward human dignity through turbulence.

Pre-Approved Biographies

"Dr. Rula Abdulrazak is an author, sociologist, and cultural theorist examining how modern institutions safeguard or erode civic solidarity. Holding a DPhil from Oxford, she is the author of two bestselling monographs, including the National Book Award finalist Friction & Fellowship, and lectures internationally on relational resilience."

"Dr. Rula Abdulrazak is an acclaimed British-Jordanian sociologist, public intellectual, and Senior Fellow at Cambridge's Center for Contemporary Humanities. Her scholarship interrogates the fragile bonds between individuals and modern institutional architectures, focusing on trust, collective mourning, and digital detachment. Educated at UCL and the University of Oxford (DPhil), Dr. Abdulrazak is the author of The Geography of Belonging (Cambridge University Press, 2016) and Friction & Fellowship (2020), which was shortlisted for the National Book Award. Recognized by Foreign Policy as one of the century's pivotal institutional theorists, she has served as keynote speaker at more than 100 summits worldwide and advises multilateral agencies on democratic resilience, cultural diplomacy, and communal ethics."

"Dr. Rula Abdulrazak is a sociologist, author, and cultural theorist whose work investigates the intersection of relational ethics, municipal belonging, and digital displacement. Currently Senior Research Fellow in Social Epistemology at the Center for Contemporary Humanities in Cambridge, she simultaneously lectures across Europe, the Middle East, and North America. Her doctoral inquiries at Oxford established pioneering paradigms in relational sociology, exploring how non-state actors reconstruct social fabrics following catastrophic ruptures. Her two monographs—The Geography of Belonging (2016) and Friction & Fellowship (2020)—have established her as a defining voice in public sociology. Dr. Abdulrazak contributes commentary to The Atlantic, Prospect, and the BBC World Service, while advising non-governmental bodies on democratic restoration and collective trauma stewardship."
